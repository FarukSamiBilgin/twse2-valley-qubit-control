# Extended abstract

**Status: Manuscript in preparation.**

Twisted WSe₂ now offers two competing routes to valley qubits: a gate-defined moiré quantum dot whose 1S valley doublet is split and driven entirely electrically [Saleem et al., arXiv:2608.05229], and an optically addressed alternative assembled from established ingredients: spin–valley Kramers qubits on the sites of the ν = 1 moiré Mott insulator. I compare the two control paradigms quantitatively in the same material, on a single continuum-model pipeline validated against two published anchors of one shared parametrization: the Devakul–Fu magic angle (predicted 1.42° vs. published 1.43°) and the gate-dot proposal itself, whose exponential valley-splitting ladder the solver reproduces [Δ₁S(W = 5/7/10) = 382/55.3/3.41 µeV against the published "hundreds / tens / a few" µeV; fitted mass 0.483 mₑ vs. 0.50 mₑ; 14/14 checks].

The comparison is not angle-symmetric: the Mott parameter collapses from U/W₁ ~ 10²–10³ at 1.43° to 2.7 at 5.08°, so each paradigm owns a different twist angle of the same crystal. A three-level Lindblad budget for the optical π gate (two-laser Raman via the moiré-trapped intralayer trion) shows that with branching ratio β = 1 the gate reaches 0.22 ns at 4×10⁻³ error and β = 0.3 is nearly as good (0.24 ns, 7×10⁻³), but the error climbs to ≳ 2×10⁻² at β ≤ 0.1; counting non-radiative trion decay as leakage raises these errors by up to a factor of 2. The focal laser power these gates need is set by the trion's transition dipole, tied here to its radiative lifetime: for lifetimes of 10 ps to 1 ns (d ≈ 25–350 D) the fastest working points need 0.02–11 mW per site, and only at the weak-dipole end does a 1-mW cap reintroduce a β penalty (β = 1: 1.2 ns, 6×10⁻³; β = 0.3: 3.1 ns, 1.5×10⁻²).

Because spin–valley locking leaves no closed Λ system at B ∥ z, engineering β into the ≳ 0.1–0.3 range is the enabling technology, and a Voigt field alone cannot supply it (β ≲ 4×10⁻³ even at 30 T against the 0.46-eV spin–orbit splitting). Twist disorder enters as an explicit axis: at a specification target σθ = 0.036° it costs the electrical architecture a 4.5% spread of Δ₁S and the optical one ~17 frequency channels, but at the disorder actually measured in tWSe₂ both budgets fail, making twist homogeneity a shared prerequisite.

Verdict: electrical control wins on maturity and disorder robustness today and matches the optical gate in speed without a laser-power budget; optical control wins the wiring scaling at N ≳ 10²–10³ qubits only if β, single-shot readout and σθ ≲ 0.05° homogeneity are all solved and the trapped trion's radiative lifetime keeps the per-gate laser power in the mW class. Neither side's T₂ is measured or reliably computable, which bounds what can be claimed.
